36  References and Further Reading

This page collects the main references used throughout Mathematical Foundations of Natural Language Processing. The list is intentionally broad: it includes classical NLP, probability and information theory, linear algebra and retrieval, neural language models, attention and transformers, retrieval-augmented generation, evaluation, optimization, graph methods, topology, and responsible NLP.

36.1 Core NLP references

For a broad introduction to natural language processing, the most useful starting points are the classical statistical treatment of Manning and Schuetze, the modern textbook by Jurafsky and Martin, the mathematically clear introduction by Eisenstein, and the neural NLP text by Goldberg (Manning and Schuetze 1999; Jurafsky and Martin 2024; Eisenstein 2019; Goldberg 2017).

36.2 Probability and information theory

The probability, likelihood, entropy, cross-entropy, KL divergence, and perplexity ideas used throughout the book are rooted in Shannon’s communication theory and standard information-theory treatments (Shannon 1948; Cover and Thomas 2006; MacKay 2003). For probabilistic modeling and statistical learning background, see Bishop, Murphy, and Hastie–Tibshirani–Friedman (Bishop 2006a; Murphy 2022; Hastie et al. 2009).

36.3 Vector spaces, retrieval, and document geometry

The document-vector view of language connects vector-space indexing, latent semantic analysis, and probabilistic retrieval (Salton et al. 1975; Deerwester et al. 1990; Landauer and Dumais 1997; Robertson and Zaragoza 2009; Manning et al. 2008). These sources support the chapters on document-term matrices, TF-IDF, cosine similarity, low-rank approximation, and retrieval.

36.4 Tokenization and word embeddings

Subword tokenization is central to modern NLP systems (Sennrich et al. 2016; Kudo and Richardson 2018). Word representations connect neural prediction, matrix factorization, and distributional semantics (Bengio et al. 2003; Mikolov, Chen, et al. 2013; Mikolov, Sutskever, et al. 2013; Pennington et al. 2014; Levy and Goldberg 2014).

36.5 Sequence models, attention, and transformers

The sequence-modeling arc begins with recurrent networks, LSTM/GRU memory mechanisms, and attention-based encoder-decoder models (Elman 1990; Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997; Cho et al. 2014; Bahdanau et al. 2015; Luong et al. 2015). Transformers and large language models build on self-attention, positional structure, pretraining, scaling, and instruction tuning (Vaswani et al. 2017; Devlin et al. 2019; Radford et al. 2019; Brown et al. 2020; Kaplan et al. 2020; Ouyang et al. 2022a).

36.6 Retrieval-augmented generation and semantic search

The retrieval and RAG chapters use ideas from sentence embeddings, dense retrieval, late interaction, large-scale similarity search, and retrieval-augmented generation (Reimers and Gurevych 2019; Karpukhin et al. 2020; Khattab and Zaharia 2020; Johnson et al. 2019; Lewis et al. 2020).

36.7 Optimization and training

Gradient-based training, stochastic optimization, recurrent-gradient pathologies, Adam, AdamW, and preference-based fine-tuning are supported by the following sources (Goodfellow et al. 2016a; Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004; Pascanu et al. 2013; Kingma and Ba 2015a; Loshchilov and Hutter 2019a; Christiano et al. 2017; Rafailov et al. 2023a).

36.8 Evaluation and calibration

The evaluation chapter uses classical overlap metrics, probabilistic scoring, calibration diagnostics, and modern model comparison ideas (Papineni et al. 2002; Lin 2004; Brier 1950; Guo et al. 2017). These references are not a substitute for task-specific human evaluation, but they provide a mathematical starting point.

36.9 Graphs and topology

Graph-based language models, PageRank, graph neural networks, and graph attention are connected to matrix operators on graphs (Page et al. 1999; Kipf and Welling 2017a; Hamilton et al. 2017; Velickovic et al. 2018). The topological viewpoint uses computational topology, persistent homology, and Mapper-style summaries (Edelsbrunner and Harer 2010; Carlsson 2009; Ghrist 2008; Singh et al. 2007).

36.10 Ethics, bias, and responsibility

The final chapter connects technical NLP modeling with fairness, bias measurement, documentation, and responsible deployment (Barocas et al. 2019a; Dwork et al. 2012; Hardt et al. 2016a; Bolukbasi, Chang, Zou, Saligrama, and Adam T. Kalai 2016; Caliskan et al. 2017; Blodgett et al. 2020; Bender et al. 2021a; Gebru et al. 2021; Mitchell et al. 2019).

This page collects the main references used throughout *Mathematical Foundations of Natural Language Processing*. The list is intentionally broad: it includes classical NLP, probability and information theory, linear algebra and retrieval, neural language models, attention and transformers, retrieval-augmented generation, evaluation, optimization, graph methods, topology, and responsible NLP.

## Core NLP references

For a broad introduction to natural language processing, the most useful starting points are the classical statistical treatment of Manning and Schuetze, the modern textbook by Jurafsky and Martin, the mathematically clear introduction by Eisenstein, and the neural NLP text by Goldberg [@manning_schuetze_1999; @jurafsky_martin_slp3; @eisenstein_2019; @goldberg_2017].

## Probability and information theory

The probability, likelihood, entropy, cross-entropy, KL divergence, and perplexity ideas used throughout the book are rooted in Shannon's communication theory and standard information-theory treatments [@shannon_1948; @cover_thomas_2006; @mackay_2003]. For probabilistic modeling and statistical learning background, see Bishop, Murphy, and Hastie--Tibshirani--Friedman [@bishop_prml_2006; @murphy_probabilistic_2022; @hastie_tibshirani_friedman_2009].

## Vector spaces, retrieval, and document geometry

The document-vector view of language connects vector-space indexing, latent semantic analysis, and probabilistic retrieval [@salton_wong_yang_1975; @deerwester_lsa_1990; @landauer_dumais_1997; @robertson_zaragoza_2009; @manning_ir_2008]. These sources support the chapters on document-term matrices, TF-IDF, cosine similarity, low-rank approximation, and retrieval.

## Tokenization and word embeddings

Subword tokenization is central to modern NLP systems [@sennrich_bpe_2016; @kudo_sentencepiece_2018]. Word representations connect neural prediction, matrix factorization, and distributional semantics [@bengio_neural_2003; @mikolov_word2vec_2013; @mikolov_distributed_2013; @pennington_glove_2014; @levy_neural_2014].

## Sequence models, attention, and transformers

The sequence-modeling arc begins with recurrent networks, LSTM/GRU memory mechanisms, and attention-based encoder-decoder models [@elman_1990; @hochreiter_lstm_1997; @cho_gru_2014; @bahdanau_attention_2015; @luong_attention_2015]. Transformers and large language models build on self-attention, positional structure, pretraining, scaling, and instruction tuning [@vaswani_attention_2017; @devlin_bert_2019; @radford_gpt2_2019; @brown_gpt3_2020; @kaplan_scaling_2020; @ouyang_instructgpt_2022].

## Retrieval-augmented generation and semantic search

The retrieval and RAG chapters use ideas from sentence embeddings, dense retrieval, late interaction, large-scale similarity search, and retrieval-augmented generation [@reimers_sentencebert_2019; @karpukhin_dpr_2020; @khattab_colbert_2020; @johnson_faiss_2019; @lewis_rag_2020].

## Optimization and training

Gradient-based training, stochastic optimization, recurrent-gradient pathologies, Adam, AdamW, and preference-based fine-tuning are supported by the following sources [@goodfellow_deep_learning_2016; @boyd_vandenberghe_2004; @pascanu_gradient_2013; @kingma_adam_2015; @loshchilov_adamw_2019; @christiano_preferences_2017; @rafailov_dpo_2023].

## Evaluation and calibration

The evaluation chapter uses classical overlap metrics, probabilistic scoring, calibration diagnostics, and modern model comparison ideas [@papineni_bleu_2002; @lin_rouge_2004; @brier_1950; @guo_calibration_2017]. These references are not a substitute for task-specific human evaluation, but they provide a mathematical starting point.

## Graphs and topology

Graph-based language models, PageRank, graph neural networks, and graph attention are connected to matrix operators on graphs [@page_pagerank_1999; @kipf_gcn_2017; @hamilton_graphsage_2017; @velickovic_gat_2018]. The topological viewpoint uses computational topology, persistent homology, and Mapper-style summaries [@edelsbrunner_harer_2010; @carlsson_topology_2009; @ghrist_barcodes_2008; @singh_mapper_2007].

## Ethics, bias, and responsibility

The final chapter connects technical NLP modeling with fairness, bias measurement, documentation, and responsible deployment [@barocas_fairness_2019; @dwork_fairness_2012; @hardt_equality_2016; @bolukbasi_debiasing_2016; @caliskan_semantics_2017; @blodgett_bias_2020; @bender_stochastic_2021; @gebru_datasheets_2021; @mitchell_modelcards_2019].
